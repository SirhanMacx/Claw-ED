# Causes of the Civil War: A Document-Based Lesson

## Lesson Overview
**Grade:** 8 | **Subject:** US History | **Duration:** 45 minutes

## Objective
Students will analyze primary and secondary sources to identify the political, economic, and social causes that led to the Civil War.

## Key Vocabulary
- **Sectionalism**: Loyalty to one's own region of the country rather than the nation as a whole
- **Abolitionist**: A person who wanted to end slavery
- **Secession**: The act of formally withdrawing from a political union
- **Popular sovereignty**: The idea that settlers in a territory should vote on whether to allow slavery
- **Compromise**: An agreement in which each side gives up something it wants

## Do Now
Look at the map showing free states and slave states in 1850.

1. Which section of the country had more slave states?
2. Why would the balance between free and slave states matter in Congress?

## Document Analysis

### Document A: Frederick Douglass, "What to the Slave is the Fourth of July?" (1852)

"What, to the American slave, is your Fourth of July? I answer: a day that reveals to him, more than all other days in the year, the gross injustice and cruelty to which he is the constant victim."

**Questions:**
1. Who is Douglass's audience?
2. What is his main argument?
3. How does he use the Fourth of July to make his point?

### Document B: John C. Calhoun, Speech to the Senate (1850)

"The South asks for justice, simple justice, and less she ought not to take. She has no compromise to offer but the Constitution, and no concession or surrender to make."

**Questions:**
1. What does Calhoun mean by "justice" for the South?
2. Why does he mention the Constitution?
3. How does his view differ from Douglass's?

### Document C: The Kansas-Nebraska Act Map (1854)

[Map showing Kansas and Nebraska territories with popular sovereignty designation]

**Questions:**
1. What principle did this act introduce?
2. Why did this lead to violence in Kansas?

## Guided Practice
Complete the evidence chart: For each cause of the Civil War (slavery, sectionalism, states' rights, economic differences), find one piece of evidence from the documents.

## Exit Ticket
Using evidence from at least TWO documents, explain: Was the Civil War inevitable, or could it have been prevented? Write 3-5 sentences using the TEA framework (Topic sentence, Evidence, Analysis).
